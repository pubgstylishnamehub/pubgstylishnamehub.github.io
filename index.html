How to Change Your Name in PUBG Mobile

Your PUBG Mobile name appears everywhere: in the lobby, on the kill feed, on your clan page and next to every chicken dinner. The name you picked when you first installed the game may not suit you any more — maybe it was a rushed choice, maybe your clan wants matching tags, or maybe you have found a stylish version you like better. Changing it is quick, but it is not free-form: PUBG Mobile only lets you rename with a special item called a Rename Card. This guide covers where that card comes from, the exact steps to use it, and how to make sure your new name is accepted the first time.

What you need: a Rename Card

A Rename Card is a single-use item stored in your inventory. One card equals one name change, and the card is consumed the moment your new name is confirmed. Your very first name, chosen when you create your account, is free — every change after that needs a card. There is no way around this inside the game, so if a website or a stranger claims they can rename your account without one, they cannot; never hand your login details to anyone offering that.

Where to get a Rename Card

There are a few honest ways to get one:

  • Progress missions. As you level up your account, the progress mission track rewards a Rename Card at certain early levels. If you have never claimed yours, check the missions screen before spending anything.
  • The in-game shop. The Rename Card is sold in the shop's items section for UC. This is the fastest route if you need a name change today.
  • Events and crew challenges. Limited-time events and crew activities occasionally include a Rename Card among their rewards. It is worth a quick look at the events tab before buying one.

Once you have the card, it sits in your inventory until you use it — it does not expire while it waits, so there is no rush to decide on a name.

Step by step: applying your new name

  1. Open PUBG Mobile and, from the main lobby, tap Inventory at the bottom of the screen.
  2. Tap the box-shaped icon at the bottom of the inventory to open your stored items, and find the Rename Card.
  3. Select the card and tap Use. A text field appears where you can enter your new name.
  4. Type or paste your new name. If you prepared a stylish name with our stylish name generator, paste it here directly from your clipboard.
  5. Check the preview, then tap OK to confirm. Your Rename Card is used and the new name takes effect immediately.

Your friends list, clan page and profile update straight away. Note that your account ID number never changes — only the display name does — so your friends can always find you by ID even after a rename.

The naming rules to respect

PUBG Mobile checks every new name before accepting it. Names must be between 3 and 16 characters long, and every letter, number, symbol and space counts toward that total. Names must also be unique across the game, which is why adding a symbol or a clan tag often turns a taken name into an available one. Offensive words, impersonation of staff or developers, and anything caught by the game's filter will be rejected — and a name that slips through can still be reported later and force-changed, costing you the card. Finally, not every exotic character renders in the game's font; a character the game cannot display may appear as a box or cause the name to be refused.

Before you spend the card: a quick checklist

Because each change costs a card, it pays to prepare. First, draft your name in the generator on our home page and copy the exact style you want, rather than trying to type symbols from memory. Second, count the characters — decorated styles grow quickly, and a name over 16 characters will not fit; shorter base names leave more room for decoration, as explained in our guide to PUBG symbols. Third, read the name once more for typos and for anything that could read as offensive out of context. If you are still deciding what the name should say, the PUBG name ideas guide has cool, funny and pro-style suggestions to start from.

Common problems

If the game says the name is already in use, add a distinguishing touch — a number, a symbol at the end, or your clan tag in brackets. If it says the name contains invalid characters, one of your symbols is not supported; remove symbols one at a time, or fall back to a plainer font style such as bold or small caps, which are widely supported. And if you cannot find your Rename Card, make sure you are looking in the box section of the inventory rather than among your outfits and weapon skins.

With a card in hand and a name prepared, the whole change takes under a minute — and you will be dropping into your next match with a name that finally looks the part.

How to Choose a Good PUBG Name

Your PUBG Mobile name is the closest thing you have to a reputation. It is what flashes on the kill feed when you win a fight, what your teammates shout in voice chat when they need help, and what strangers remember — or forget — after a match. Because every change after your first one costs a Rename Card, choosing well the first time is worth a few minutes of thought. This guide walks through what actually makes a name good, and a simple process for landing on one you will still like in six months.

What a good name has to do

A good in-game name does four jobs at once. It has to be readable at a glance in a fast-moving kill feed. It has to be easy to say out loud, because "the player with the unpronounceable symbols" is useless as a callout when your squad is pinned down. It has to be findable, so friends can search for you and send a request without copying characters one by one. And it has to feel like you — a name borrowed from a famous player or a random list works for a week, then starts to feel like someone else's jacket. If a candidate name fails any of these four tests, keep looking.

Keep it short

PUBG Mobile names can be between 3 and 16 characters, and every letter, number, symbol and space counts. The sweet spot for the name itself is four to eight letters. A name in that range stays readable, leaves room for a clan tag or a pair of decorations from the PUBG symbols library, and does not get cut off or squeezed on smaller screens. Long names also age badly: the longer the name, the more likely one part of it stops feeling right. If your favourite word is long, consider trimming it — dropping vowels or using a recognised short form keeps the meaning and frees up characters.

Make it easy to say and type

Before you commit to a name, run two quick tests. The voice test: say it out loud as if you are calling for help mid-fight. If you stumble over it in your own room, teammates will stumble over it under pressure. The search test: imagine a friend typing it into the game's friend search from memory. Names built entirely from symbols fail both tests, and names with five different spellings of the same sound fail the second one. One or two decorated characters are fine — a crown at the front is easy to describe — but the core of the name should be plain, typeable letters.

Start from something that is yours

The names that last are built from material that means something to the player: an animal you like, weather you grew up with, a place, a hobby, an inside joke from your squad. Start by listing five to ten words connected to you, then sharpen them. Combine two short words (a creature plus a material, a colour plus a weapon, a place plus a storm), swap a letter for a similar-looking one, or add a number that matters to you rather than a random string of digits. Our PUBG name ideas guide shows this building process across cool, funny and pro styles, and it is a good place to gather raw material before you narrow down.

Pick a tone and commit to it

Decide what you want the name to say before you decide the exact words. Fierce names lean on predators, steel and storms. Funny names lean on the game's own jokes — bad aim, bad luck, the eternal frying pan. Clean, pro-style names are usually one strong word with nothing wasted. Problems start when a name tries to be all three at once and ends up muddled. Our guides to one-word PUBG names and names with attitude explore two opposite ends of that spectrum if you want to see how different tones change the same basic idea.

Check it before you spend anything

When you have a favourite, check three things. First, availability: PUBG names must be unique, so your exact pick may already belong to someone else — our guide on why a PUBG name shows as taken explains the usual reasons and the cleanest fixes. Second, rendering: type the styled version into the generator, and remember that some fancy Unicode characters show as boxes on some devices, which the fonts and Unicode safety guide covers in detail. Third, judgement: read the name once as a stranger would. Does it read as offensive out of context, or look like an attempt to copy a known player? If so, adjust now, not after the card is spent.

Dress it up last

Choose the word first and the decoration last. Once the plain name is settled, run it through the stylish name generator and compare fonts side by side — bold and small caps are the safest, script and fraktur the most dramatic. Add at most one matched pair of symbols so the name looks designed rather than decorated by accident. Then follow the steps in our guide to changing your name in PUBG Mobile, check the in-game preview one final time, and confirm. A name chosen this way — short, sayable, personal and checked — is one you will not be itching to replace next season. For more help, browse the full list of guides.

Adding symbols, step by step

Where symbols can go

Placement changes the whole feel of a name. A matched pair around the name — wings on both sides, or the same star at each end — looks designed and symmetrical. The truth about spaces

Here is the part many guides skip: a normal keyboard space is usually rejected in the PUBG Mobile rename field, which is why so many stylish names run their words together or use capital letters to separate them, like ShadowFox. Invisible (Blank) Name in PUBG Mobile: Does It Still Work?

Few name tricks generate more videos and more disappointment than the invisible name — a PUBG profile that appears to have no name at all. How the blank name trick works in theory

A PUBG name cannot literally be empty — the game requires between 3 and 16 characters. The trick therefore never used "nothing"; it used special characters that occupy a slot but draw nothing visible, such as certain blank Braille patterns, Hangul filler characters, or combinations of marks that render as empty space. Pasted into the rename field, a string of these could pass the length check while displaying as a blank. Our guide to symbols and spaces explains the same characters in their milder use, as gaps between words.

What happens when you try it now

In current versions, most attempts end in one of three ways. The game rejects the name outright as invalid, because the accepted-character list no longer includes the blank characters being pasted. The game accepts the paste but trims or replaces the blanks when saving, leaving you with a fragment or an error at confirmation. Or — the outcome that causes the most confusion — the name appears blank on your own phone but shows as boxes, question marks or placeholder characters on other players' devices, because rendering depends on each device's font, as our fonts and Unicode guide details. Reports from players change with every major update, which is exactly the point: even when a particular blank character slips through after one patch, the next patch commonly blocks it. No guide can honestly promise you a working invisible name today, and any video or website that guarantees one is describing the past or selling you a risk.

A wasted Rename Card. If a blank name is accepted and later force-reset by the game, the card is gone; you will need another to choose a proper name. Being unfindable. Friends cannot search for a name they cannot see or type, and teammates cannot call you out — in a squad game, an invisible name is a practical handicap, not just a cosmetic one. Reporting friction. Both directions suffer: other players struggle to report a blank name, and if your name is flagged, an account whose identity is a blank string invites more scrutiny, not less. Clan and tournament problems. Organised play requires identifiable names; a blank name can make you ineligible or simply impossible to credit for your own plays.

All three survive updates, display on every device, and keep you searchable by the friends you actually play with.

The bottom line

The invisible name belongs to an earlier era of PUBG Mobile. The characters it relied on are largely blocked, the results vary by device even when a name slips through, and the costs — a possibly wasted Rename Card, an unfindable profile, extra attention from reports — are real, while the benefit is a novelty that lasts until the next patch. PUBG Fonts and Unicode Symbols: Which Devices Show Them?

Here is the honest truth behind every stylish name: there is no guarantee that what looks perfect on your phone looks the same on everyone else's. Stylish names are built from Unicode characters, and whether a character displays properly depends on the fonts installed on each device and the font the game itself uses. This guide explains why some names turn into boxes or blanks, which styles are safest, and — most importantly — how to test a name properly before you spend a Rename Card on it.

Why boxes and blanks appear

Unicode defines well over a hundred thousand characters: alphabets, mathematical letters, circled and squared letters, small caps, symbols from many writing systems. No single font contains all of them. When an app meets a character its font does not include, it shows a placeholder — an empty rectangle players call a "box" or "tofu", a question mark, or occasionally nothing at all. PUBG Mobile ships with its own font coverage, which handles the popular name decorations well, but your phone's system font, your friend's older Android, and an iPhone may each cover slightly different sets. That is why a name can look flawless in the generator on your device, perfect in your own game, and still show as boxes to the teammate whose screen matters. The name itself is never broken — it is the same text everywhere — only the rendering differs.

Which styles are safest

Styles fall into rough safety tiers. The safest tier is the game's own long-established decorations: crowns, stars, the ꧁༺ ༻꧂ wings and simple brackets from the symbols library, which PUBG's font has supported for years, plus plain letters in bold and small caps, which are extremely widely covered. The middle tier — script, italic, monospace, circled letters — displays correctly on most modern devices but can fail on older phones or in some overlays. The risky tier is the exotic end: squared letters, the "aesthetic" vowel-heavy alphabets, rare combining marks and obscure symbols. These are the styles most likely to collapse into boxes somewhere. None of this makes the risky styles forbidden — it makes them styles to test, which the next section covers.

How to test a name before spending a Rename Card

Use the rename preview properly. Paste your candidate into the rename field and study the preview on the game's own screen before confirming, following the rename steps. If a character shows as a box there, it will show as a box in matches — cancel and simplify.

Check on a second device. If anyone in your household or squad uses a different phone brand or an older model, send them the name in chat and ask how it looks, or view your profile from their account. Two different devices agreeing is strong evidence.

Test the characters in isolation. If a long decorated name fails, paste its symbols one at a time into a chat message or your clan description to find which single character is unsupported, then swap just that one.

Prefer a fallback plan. Decide in advance what your name becomes if a character fails — usually the same word in bold or small caps with one safe symbol. Having the fallback ready means a failed test costs you two minutes, not a card.

The special case of blank and invisible characters

Some characters render as nothing on one device and as a box on another, which is why names built from blank characters are the least predictable of all. Our guide to the invisible name trick covers that topic honestly: the characters are largely blocked at the rename screen now, and results vary even when they are accepted. The same caution applies to spaces, as explained in our guide to adding symbols and spaces — a gap that looks clean on your phone may be a rectangle on someone else's.

Rules change with updates

One last honest point: font support is not fixed forever. Game updates add characters, remove others, and change what the rename filter accepts, so a name that tested perfectly last season is not guaranteed forever — and a name on your account stays as it was saved, even if a later update would no longer accept it. The practical approach is the one this guide opened with: choose from the safe tiers when the name matters, test anything exotic before committing, and keep decoration simple enough that one unsupported character cannot ruin the whole name. Do that, and your stylish name will look as good on your squad's screens as it does on yours. For more, browse the guides index.
